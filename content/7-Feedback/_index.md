### Overall Evaluation

At this stage, the First Cloud AI Journey program has given me the opportunity to approach AWS through a combination of foundational knowledge, hands-on labs, and a team project. The learning process goes beyond using individual services and helps me understand how to select, connect, and operate components within a cloud architecture.

**1. Learning environment and working format**  
The program combines approximately ten in-person sessions with predominantly self-directed study at home. The in-person sessions are useful for discussing issues with mentors, meeting other participants, and resolving topics that are difficult to communicate through documentation alone. Self-study provides flexibility but also requires proactive planning, progress management, and independent technical research.

**2. Support from mentors and program coordinators**  
The mentors and program coordinators provide direction, reference materials, and assistance during the learning process. I appreciate that mentors suggest an analytical approach and allow participants to experiment and identify the cause of an issue instead of only providing an answer. This approach improves both troubleshooting skills and the ability to explain technical problems.

**3. Relevance of the program content**  
The curriculum progresses from AWS fundamentals to more systematic hands-on exercises. I have explored AWS CLI, EC2, S3, CloudFront, RDS, ALB, Auto Scaling, CloudWatch, Route 53 Resolver, IAM, and other related services. Combining these topics with a project helps turn individual concepts into practical architectural knowledge.

**4. Technical skill development**  
The worklogs and labs have improved my ability to read documentation, configure resources, validate connectivity, inspect logs, and troubleshoot errors. Through the **fcaj-rag-chat** project, I have studied the Kotaemon architecture, Docker Compose, Gemini, ChromaDB, LanceDB, and SQLite while preparing a deployment plan based on Amazon ECR, Amazon ECS Fargate, and Amazon EFS. At this stage, the project is being configured and tested locally in preparation for AWS deployment.

**5. Teamwork and reporting skills**  
Weekly worklogs help me record objectives, tasks, references, and results in a structured way. During the project, the team needs to discuss architecture, divide responsibilities, report progress, and agree on test criteria. This provides an opportunity to practice clear communication and evidence-based collaboration.

**6. Program policies and participation conditions**  
The internship program does not provide an allowance. The combination of in-person sessions and self-study at home offers scheduling flexibility, but the outcome depends heavily on each participant's initiative. Additional AWS sandbox access, lab credits, or detailed cost-control guidance would make it easier to practice services that may incur charges.

### Additional Questions

**What have you found most satisfying during the program?**

The most satisfying part is the progression from studying individual AWS services to analyzing a real project. Through **fcaj-rag-chat**, I have gained a clearer understanding of how a RAG application processes documents, creates embeddings, builds indexes, retrieves context, and generates answers with citations. Designing how to containerize the application and map its architecture to ECR, ECS Fargate, and EFS has helped me understand the relationship among the application, persistent data, and cloud infrastructure.

**What could the program improve for future interns?**

The program could provide a clearer weekly roadmap in which each phase has specific objectives, deliverables, and completion criteria. In addition to self-study materials, short mentor checkpoints, an architecture review before deployment, and AWS cost-management guidance would help participants avoid overly broad learning paths and identify design problems earlier.

**Would you recommend the program to a friend? Why?**

Yes. I would recommend the program to people who want to learn AWS through theory, labs, and a practical project. However, participants should be prepared for substantial self-study because most work is completed at home. The program is best suited to proactive learners who are willing to research documentation, record their practice, and discuss issues when they need support.

---

### Suggestions and Expectations

**Suggestions for improving the program experience:**

* Provide a weekly roadmap, deliverable checklist, and consistent evaluation criteria from the beginning.
* Hold periodic checkpoints so mentors can review progress, architecture decisions, and technical issues.
* Add guidance on AWS Budgets, cost alerts, resource cleanup, and safe resource usage.
* Consider providing an AWS sandbox or lab credits for exercises that may incur charges.
* Organize short sharing sessions where each team can present its architecture, demonstrate results, and discuss lessons learned.
* Provide common templates for evidence, test cases, and incident reports to make weekly worklogs more consistent.

**Would you like to continue with this program in the future?**

Yes. If given the opportunity, I would like to continue developing my AWS deployment, monitoring, cost-optimization, and security skills. I would also like to continue evolving the project from a local application into a system that is fully deployed and tested in a cloud environment.

**Any other comments?**

The program has helped me understand that learning cloud computing is not only about knowing the names of services or how to create them. It also requires attention to security, cost, operability, persistent data, and testing. Combining self-study, labs, worklogs, and a team project creates a highly practical learning path. I appreciate the guidance and opportunities for discussion that I have received and hope the program continues to strengthen its technical checkpoints so participants can turn separate pieces of knowledge into a complete and verifiable solution.
